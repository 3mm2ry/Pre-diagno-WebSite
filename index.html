The Challenge We Face

Public healthcare in Bahrain faces significant challenges in emergency department triage and patient flow management.

Overcrowding

Severe overcrowding in public healthcare institutions

Long Waiting Times

Extended waiting periods for patients in emergency rooms

Prioritization Difficulty

Challenges in accurately prioritizing urgent cases quickly

Delayed Critical Care

Minor cases inadvertently delaying care for critical patients

Project Aim & Objectives

Establishing the groundwork for an optimized pre-diagnosis system.

Project Aim

To design and develop an AI-based pre-diagnosis mobile application that improves emergency responsiveness in Bahrain's public healthcare institutions by prioritizing cases and streamlining the initial triage phase.

Research Objective 1 (RO1)

Identify the key challenges in the current emergency triage process within Bahrain's public hospitals.

Research Objective 2 (RO2)

Determine the functional and non-functional requirements for an AI-driven mobile pre-diagnosis solution to address identified triage inefficiencies.

Research Question Data Sources Research Method
RQ1: What are the primary bottlenecks in the current ER triage? Literature, Hospital Staff Qualitative (Interviews), Literature Review
RQ2: How can AI facilitate acuity categorization before hospital arrival? System Design Analysis, Prototyping Quantitative (Usability Testing), Action Research

Literature Review

Foundation built on extensive research and market analysis.

Systematic Literature Review (SLR)

Extensive review of existing academic literature focusing on AI applications in healthcare, pre-diagnosis algorithms, and emergency triage optimizations. The review established the effectiveness of NLP in symptom extraction and the viability of mobile pre-assessment in reducing ER bottlenecks.

Comparative Analysis (CA)

Analysis of existing digital health applications revealed significant gaps: lack of integration with national systems (eKey), absence of localized Arabic/regional NLP models for symptoms, and missing automated pathways from pre-diagnosis to disposition (appointment or teleconsultation).

Conclusion

Successfully designed an AI-based pre-diagnosis framework tailored for Bahrain.

Demonstrated significant potential to reduce ER waiting times and overcrowding.

Achieved high usability scores (92.7%) during evaluation phases.

Established a clear, actionable workflow from symptom input to disposition.

Validated the integration of localized AI symptom analysis with national identity systems.
